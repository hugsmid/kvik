# 4.8 Commit latency and throughput of the log writer

Roadmap item 4.8. The harness is [`log/examples/commit_latency.rs`](../../log/examples/commit_latency.rs); it drives
the engine's own writer (`kvik-log`, roadmap 4.4 and 4.5) on a real file system. This page records macOS, a
development platform with no budget, and the Linux NVMe target, which checks the delivery budget of
[`design.md`](../design.md#budgets-to-be-measured) (see Linux NVMe target). Cloud-runner figures for Linux and
Windows are recorded under Cloud runners, labelled
as not durability-representative.

## Summary

- **macOS, timed mark sync, quiet and 10 × 1 Hz loads (either bound) and 100 × 1 Hz (no bound):** p50 4.1–4.2 ms
  and p99 5.2–6.4 ms per acknowledged call. The durable sync (`F_FULLFSYNC`) alone takes 4.0 ms
  (p50), so the engine adds about 0.1–0.2 ms to it. That includes the first reading after a quiet period.
- **Against the 2.2 spike** (100 staggered 1 Hz series, no window, mark timed on the writer thread): p50 4.17,
  p99 5.22, p99.9 9.00 and max 9.48 ms, against the spike's 3.93, 4.86, 8.91 and 9.61 ms. At 1,000 calls a second
  with no bound the two agree as closely (5.36 / 8.38 / 12.41 / 16.39 ms against 5.71 / 8.17 / 12.68 / 16.04 ms).
- **The `min_sync_interval` statement of `design.md` holds here.** At 1,000 calls a second the default of 10 ms
  caps syncs at 99 a second (10 calls per batch) and p50 rises from 5.36 to 8.27 ms: the sync plus about half the
  interval. p99 stays at 13.4 ms.
- **At exactly 100 calls a second** (100 staggered 1 Hz series, 10 ms apart, the load `design.md` names) the bound
  leaves p50 at the sync (4.14 ms) but lifts p90 to 12.0 and p99 to 14.1 ms. Once a call arrives a little after
  the interval allows, the writer sleeps out the rest of the interval, the sleep overshoots, so the next call waits
  longer, until two calls share a batch (94 syncs a second, 1.1 calls per batch). Only a wake with no lateness at
  all keeps the wait as small as the first: the Linux target, with timer slack of 0.17 ms, shows a tail of the same
  size, and there it lifts p50 above 5 ms (Linux NVMe target, Q-310); how the larger slack here leaves p50 at the
  sync is not shown. Below 20 ms here either way (macOS has no budget). Since fixed: see the dated note under
  Results: macOS.
- **Mark synced at every commit** (the rule on Windows and without a boot identity) costs a second sync per commit:
  p50 8.1 ms on quiet loads, p99 up to 25.9 ms in bursts, and about a quarter less throughput under saturation than
  with the timed mark at the same bound.
- **Timed mark sync on the writer thread** (`spec.md` §3 Durable mark): a call that arrives during it waits for
  it. On quiet loads that is about 0.4 % of calls (4 ms of each second); max 8.3–8.5 ms, two syncs. Confirmed on
  macOS, as in 2.2.
- **Throughput.** One writer waiting for each acknowledgement: 249 commits a second with no bound (one per sync).
  Saturation, four writers with calls of 100 readings: 5.3 million readings a second with no bound, 4.6 million at
  10 ms, 3.5 million with the mark synced at every commit, in batches of about 50,000 readings (the 1 MiB batch
  cap). Latency there is queueing behind back-pressure (p50 19–31 ms) and is not a budget figure.
- **Restart** (Q-116 (n)): opening a store of 7–10 segments (446–639 MiB) again took 130–241 ms, against the 1 s
  budget, after a clean shutdown with a warm page cache. A store of two segments holding almost nothing took 129–138
  ms in a short run (table below the results): the time is mostly reading the two unsealed 64 MiB segments whole,
  which a cold cache will lengthen.
- **Timer slack on this run.** The operating system added half of every timed wait (a 5 ms sleep woke 2.5 ms late).
  The writer's waits for `min_sync_interval` inherit it, so the 10 ms rows that wait on the bound are slower here
  than they would be without it (back to back: 79 commits a second instead of about 100). The load generator was
  built to stay on time under it: its lag at p99 was below 1 µs in every paced row (the run did not record the
  maximum; the harness now prints it).

## Method

**What runs.** The harness opens a fresh store per writer setting with `WriterConfig::default()` (64 MiB segments
created ahead, 1 MiB batches, a queue of 65,536 entries, the disk marks), changing only `min_sync_interval` and the
mark sync, and starts `Log` on its two threads. The file system is `StdFs`, wrapped to time each sync by file
(segment, mark, directory); nothing in the writer changes. The store gets a fixed boot identity, so the timed mark
sync is in force where the row says so; the harness checks the writer's reported mode against the row's label.
Before each setup's loads one call is committed and the run waits 0.5 s, so the first segment and the one created
ahead exist.

**Latency** runs from the submit call to the acknowledgement. The acknowledgement is stamped on the writer thread:
the harness registers a waker on each call's `Pending`, and the writer wakes it as it completes the call, after the
batch's sync, the mark's move and, where required, the mark's sync. This is *ack* in 2.2's terms. The `design.md`
budget is to delivery to an in-process consumer; delivery is roadmap 5, and 2.2 found delivery at most 0.09 ms
above ack, so ack stands in for it here.

**Loads.** Each call holds one reading unless stated; a reading is an `f64` fact.

| Load | What it does | Full run |
|---|---|---|
| quiet | one writer, a call every 20–200 ms (pseudo-random, fixed seed): every call is the first after a quiet period | 40 s |
| steady *N* × 1 Hz | *N* series at 1 Hz, staggered evenly, one call per reading, open loop: *N* = 10, 100, 1,000 | 40, 20, 10 s |
| burst | every second, 500 calls submitted back to back | 20 s |
| back to back | one writer, each call submitted once the previous one is acknowledged | 10 s |
| saturation | four writers submitting calls of 100 readings as fast as back-pressure lets them | 5 s |

- Paced loads are open loop: the generator sends at fixed offsets, sleeping and then spinning, and records how late
  it sent (*Lag*). Latency counts from the submit call, so a late generator does not add to it but would bunch
  arrivals. Sleeps are split so that timer slack cannot make it late.
- A paced call that meets back-pressure is counted (*Busy*); none did.
- Every load runs under three settings: `min_sync_interval` 0 with the mark timed at 1 s; 10 ms (the default) with
  the mark timed; 10 ms with the mark synced at every commit.
- Percentiles are nearest rank; one needs enough calls to mean anything, so p90 is given from 10 calls, p99 from
  100 and p99.9 from 1,000. Calls in one batch wait for the same sync, so tails rest on the slowest few syncs.
- *Sync calls on their own* time each call through the same seam on an otherwise idle store: a 256-byte write
  then the sync, on a preallocated zero-filled file, for 2 s or 2,000 calls.
- *Opening the store again* times `StoreDir::open` and `LogWriter::open` after the clean shutdown that ends each
  setup: once with the same boot identity (rule 1) and once with another (rule 3, a recovery entry written).

**Code measured.** The engine as of the commit before this page's; later changes to recovery and the mark (a reset
stamp in the mark, rule 1's recovery entry) leave the commit path as measured, and a short run after them opened the
stores again without fault.

**Machine.** A 12-core ARM64 computer with its internal NVMe SSD, macOS 27.2, APFS, as in 2.2. The machine was in
ordinary use, load average 1.1–2.3. The run took 448 s at normal priority, one run of the full matrix after one
earlier run that had found the timer slack; the spread between runs was not measured this time (2.2: treat tails
within about ±25 % as equal). Write amplification was not measured again; 2.2's figures stand.

**Reproduce.**

```text
cargo run --release --locked -p kvik-log --example commit_latency -- <dir> --disk "internal NVMe SSD"   # about 8 minutes
cargo run --release --locked -p kvik-log --example commit_latency -- <dir> --short                      # about 2 minutes
```

`--only <load>` and `--repeat <n>` narrow or repeat the matrix. The run works in a `kvik-commit-latency`
subdirectory of `<dir>`, refuses `tmpfs` and `ramfs` (their sync does nothing), removes the stores at the end and
prints the tables below, with the environment: platform, file system, disk class (found where the system tells it,
or stated with `--disk`; never a model or serial), sync primitive, timer slack and load average.

### 2026-11-17: the mark synced at the first batch in each segment (Q-630)

The first batch in a segment is now acknowledged only after the mark naming it is synced (spec §3 Commit). What is
established is the count, pinned by `log/tests/generation/first_start.rs`
`the_first_batch_in_each_segment_forces_exactly_one_mark_sync`: one mark sync more per segment, at its first batch
and none at a timed seal, and since the forced sync restarts the timer the number of mark syncs over time does not
rise; one of them moves earlier. So one batch per segment, and the batches queued behind it, wait one more sync,
every 64 MiB or 5 minutes; on slow media that one commit takes two syncs. Its cost in time is not measured here: one
run before and one after on a shared 4-core cloud machine at load about 5 (ext4 on a virtual disk) show the same mark
syncs per load, but their latencies differ by more than one sync per segment could explain (the saturation p50
fell), so single runs at that load cannot resolve it. The figure is to come from the next run of the harness on the
target.

## Against the budgets

`design.md` sets p50 ≤ 5 ms and p99 ≤ 20 ms for Linux on an NVMe SSD, with p50 ≤ 2 ms as a stretch goal on drives
with power-loss protection, and holds neither macOS nor Windows to them. Read against those figures, as 2.2 did,
for the default setting (10 ms, mark timed):

| Load | p50 ≤ 5 ms | p99 ≤ 20 ms | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| quiet, steady 10 × 1 Hz | yes (4.2) | yes (6.1–6.2) | the first reading after a quiet period is synced at once |
| steady 100 × 1 Hz | yes (4.1) | yes (14.1) | p90 12.0, the wait for the interval building up (Summary); 5.2 p99 at 0 |
| steady 1000 × 1 Hz | no (8.3) | yes (13.4) | above 100 batches a second, as `design.md` says; 5.4 with the bound at 0 |
| burst of 500 | no (16.6) | yes (17.2) | the burst after the first batch waits for the interval, lengthened here by timer slack; 7.6 at 0 |
| back to back | no (13.0) | yes (15.0) | each commit waits for the interval, lengthened here by timer slack; 4.0 at 0 |
| saturation | no | no | queueing behind back-pressure, outside the budget's load statement |

- The stretch goal is out of reach on this machine: the sync alone takes 4.0 ms.
- With the mark synced at every commit p50 is about two syncs (8.1 ms) on the quiet and 10 × 1 Hz loads and higher
  elsewhere (9.0 ms at 100 × 1 Hz, 12.2 at 1000 × 1 Hz, 18.2 in bursts, 11.0 back to back, 30.8 under saturation); p99
  passes except in bursts (25.9 ms) and under saturation (57.6 ms). That is the Windows configuration, which has no
  budget.
- Whether the budget holds on Linux is decided by the target run (Linux NVMe target), not by these figures.

What they show: with a near-free sync the engine adds about 0.03–0.3 ms per acknowledged call at p50; with the
default 10 ms bound p50 is half the interval and p99 the interval, as `design.md` states; the mark synced at every
commit adds about 0.1 ms on quiet loads, as a second near-free sync. Opening a store of 3–5 segments took 0.24–0.68 s
with a warm page cache, more than on macOS for fewer segments; the target run took 0.39–0.74 s for 7–10 (Linux
NVMe target). The full tables are the workflow's artifacts.

## Linux NVMe target

`scripts/bench-nvme.sh` at commit b87c8e9 (its build before the rebase onto main, the same change) ran the full
matrix three times (1,334 s, normal priority):
[tables](4.8-linux-nvme-2026-11-15-201128.md). The machine: x86_64, 6 CPUs, Linux 7.0, ext4 on an NVMe SSD through a
logical volume (device mapper), consumer class with no power-loss protection, write cache write back with FUA;
`fdatasync` for batches and the mark; timer slack 0.17 ms at p50 (0.70 at most); load average 1.39 at the start.
No call failed. Ranges are over the three runs; at the default setting the paced loads' p50 agree within 10 %.

```text
scripts/bench-nvme.sh <dir on the NVMe file system> --disk "NVMe, power-loss protection: yes|no"
```

Against the budgets, default setting (10 ms, mark timed), ms:

| Load | p50 ≤ 5 ms | p99 ≤ 20 ms | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| quiet | yes (2.36–2.46) | yes (4.59–9.79) | p99 follows the slowest syncs (batch sync p99 4.48–9.68) |
| steady 10 × 1 Hz | yes (2.30–2.52) | yes (2.71–2.79) | |
| steady 100 × 1 Hz | **no (6.75–7.02)** | yes (12.24–12.33) | the wait for the interval builds up (below); Q-310 |

- **Verdict.** p99 ≤ 20 ms holds at every load the budget covers. p50 ≤ 5 ms holds on quiet and 10 × 1 Hz loads and
  is missed at exactly 100 calls a second, the edge `design.md` names (Q-310). The stretch goal does not apply (no
  power-loss protection); p50 would meet it at 1,000 calls a second with the bound at 0 (0.41 ms), not at low rates.
- **Low rates take 2.4 ms, not the 0.3 ms of the sync alone.** With the bound at 0 and the mark timed, quiet,
  10 × 1 Hz and two of three 100 × 1 Hz runs give p50 2.24–2.46 ms; 1000 × 1 Hz gives 0.41 ms. The time is inside
  `fdatasync`: the batch sync the writer times around the call alone takes 2.1–2.3 ms at p50 in those rows and
  0.34–0.35 ms at 1000 × 1 Hz, the mark's sync likewise, and latency stays about 0.1 ms above the batch sync in
  the quiet and steady rows with the bound at 0. So the writer's wake path is not the cause, nor timer slack
  (0.17 ms, and with the bound at 0 the writer sets no timer for the sync).
  The sync has two levels, about 0.35–0.5 ms and about 2.0–2.3 ms, and the level is not set by the rate or the idle
  time alone. Batch sync p50 in the three runs: 100 × 1 Hz at 0 ms 2.24, 0.54 and 2.12 ms; back to back at 0 ms,
  with no idle gap, 2.02, 0.44 and 2.10 ms; back to back at 10 ms 0.51 ms in every run, while 100 × 1 Hz at 10 ms,
  at the same 99 syncs a second, 2.16–2.20 ms; 1000 × 1 Hz with the mark synced at every commit 0.37, 2.04 and
  2.04 ms. The probe of the sync alone, one thread in a tight loop, mostly sees the fast
  level (p50 0.30, p99 2.36). Hypothesis, not tested: a state below the file system, the drive's power or cache
  state or the CPUs' idle states on an otherwise quiet machine, switches the flush between the two costs. A rerun of
  100 × 1 Hz with the drive's autonomous power states and the deep CPU idle states held off would tell. Either way
  it sits inside the budget.
- **The 10 ms bound costs latency at 100 and 1,000 calls a second, as designed.** At 1,000 it caps syncs at 99 a
  second (10 calls per batch, from 959–998) and p50 rises from 0.41 to 5.43–5.45 ms, p99 from 0.50–3.94 to
  10.34–10.42 ms: the sync plus half the interval, and the whole interval. At exactly 100 a second p50 rises from
  2.24–2.36 (0.68 in one run) to 6.75–7.02 ms. The bound counts from the previous sync's start and calls come
  exactly one interval apart, so each late timed wake (about 0.1 ms here: 99 syncs a second for 100 calls) delays
  every later sync; the wait climbs to the whole interval, then two calls share a batch. Latency then spreads
  evenly from the sync (2.2 ms) to the sync plus 10 ms, which predicts 7.2 / 11.2 / 12.2 ms at p50 / p90 / p99
  against 6.75–7.02 / 11.40–11.54 / 12.24–12.33 measured. This rests on the code and that fit, not on a trace.
  Calls arriving at random would explain it no better: the harness spaces them evenly, and random arrivals at
  this sync time would give about 73 syncs a second with 1.4 calls per batch and p50 5.4 ms (measured: 99, 1.0,
  6.75–7.02), p99 about 11 ms at 10 × 1 Hz (measured 2.71–2.79), and p50 about 7 ms on macOS (measured 4.14). Back to back gets 98 commits a second (from 637–845);
  bursts are faster with the bound (p50 10.79–10.94 against 12.42–12.70 ms).
- **Mark synced at every commit** (not the Linux setting: Linux has a boot identity, so the mark is timed) roughly
  doubles p50 at low rates, a second sync: 4.47–4.71 ms on quiet and 10 × 1 Hz against 2.30–2.52. Under saturation
  p99 reached 130.23 and 132.75 ms in runs 1 and 3 but 52.30 in run 2 (46.17–48.19 with the mark timed): in runs 1
  and 3 the batch syncs slowed (p50 4.30–4.47 against 1.01 ms, max 57–63 ms), the mark's too (max 52–56 ms), and
  throughput fell to 3.3–3.7 million readings a second against 4.7 million in run 2. These numbers do not show why
  those runs were slow; neither saturation nor this setting is held to the budget.
- **Throughput.** Saturation: 5.1–5.7 million readings a second with no bound and 4.6–4.8 million at 10 ms, about
  macOS's figures although the sync is far faster; the disk syncs about 100 batches a second of 0.9–1.0 ms, so
  the engine's own work bounds it.
- **Restart** (Q-116 (n)): opening a store of 7–10 segments (446–639 MiB) again took 392–742 ms on the same boot
  and 393–739 ms on another, after a clean shutdown with a warm page cache: inside the 1 s budget, about three
  times macOS, and not in step with size (446 MiB took 392 ms in one run and 639 ms in another). A cold cache and a
  crash are still to measure.
- **Against macOS.** The sync alone 0.30 against 4.0 ms; p50 on quiet and 10 × 1 Hz loads 2.3–2.5 against 4.1–4.2
  ms; 1,000 calls a second with no bound 0.41 against 5.36 ms; 100 × 1 Hz at 10 ms 6.75–7.02 against 4.14 ms at p50
  but 11.40–11.54 against 12.0 at p90; back to back at 10 ms 98 against 79 commits a second, the smaller slack;
  restart about three times slower.

writes, a full copy-on-write file system or power cuts, a restart from a cold page cache after a crash, and write

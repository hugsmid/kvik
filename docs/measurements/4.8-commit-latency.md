# 4.8 Commit latency and throughput of the log writer

Roadmap item 4.8. The harness is [`log/examples/commit_latency.rs`](../../log/examples/commit_latency.rs); it drives
the engine's own writer (`kvik-log`, roadmap 4.4 and 4.5) on a real file system. This page records one machine,
macOS, a development platform with no budget. **The run on the Linux NVMe target, which checks the delivery budget
of [`design.md`](../design.md#budgets-to-be-measured), is pending** (see Pending), and so are the cloud-runner
figures for Linux and Windows.

## Summary

- **macOS, timed mark sync, quiet and 10 × 1 Hz loads (either bound) and 100 × 1 Hz (no bound):** p50 4.1–4.2 ms
  and p99 5.2–6.4 ms per acknowledged call. The durable sync (`F_FULLFSYNC`) alone takes 4.0 ms
  (p50), so the engine adds about 0.1–0.2 ms to it. That includes the first reading after a quiet period.
- **Against the 2.2 spike** (100 staggered 1 Hz series, no window, mark timed on the writer thread): p50 4.17,
  p99 5.22, p99.9 9.00 and max 9.48 ms, against the spike's 3.93, 4.86, 8.91 and 9.61 ms. At 1,000 calls a second
  with no bound the two agree as closely (5.36 / 8.38 / 12.41 / 16.39 ms against 5.71 / 8.17 / 12.68 / 16.04 ms).
- **The `min_sync_interval` statement of `design.md` holds here.** At 1,000 calls a second the default of 10 ms
  caps syncs at 99 a second (10 calls per batch) and p50 rises from 5.36 to 8.27 ms: the sync plus about half the
  interval. p99 stays at 13.4 ms.
- **At exactly 100 calls a second** (100 staggered 1 Hz series, 10 ms apart, the load `design.md` names) the bound
  leaves p50 at the sync (4.14 ms) but lifts p90 to 12.0 and p99 to 14.1 ms. This tail is most likely the timer
  slack below, not the bound: once a call arrives a little after the interval allows, the writer sleeps out the rest
  of the interval, each sleep overshoots by half, so the next call waits longer, until two calls share a batch (94
  syncs a second, 1.1 calls per batch). Without slack the wait would stay as small as the first. The target run
  decides; inside the p99 budget either way.
- **Mark synced at every commit** (the rule on Windows and without a boot identity) costs a second sync per commit:
  p50 8.1 ms on quiet loads, p99 up to 25.9 ms in bursts, and about a quarter less throughput under saturation than
  with the timed mark at the same bound.
- **Timed mark sync on the writer thread** (`spec.md` §3 Durable mark): a call that arrives during it waits for
  it. On quiet loads that is about 0.4 % of calls (4 ms of each second); max 8.3–8.5 ms, two syncs. Confirmed on
  macOS, as in 2.2.
- **Throughput.** One writer waiting for each acknowledgement: 249 commits a second with no bound (one per sync).
  Saturation, four writers with calls of 100 readings: 5.3 million readings a second with no bound, 4.6 million at
  10 ms, 3.5 million with the mark synced at every commit, in batches of about 50,000 readings (the 1 MiB batch
  cap). Latency there is queueing behind back-pressure (p50 19–31 ms) and is not a budget figure.
- **Restart** (Q-116 (n)): opening a store of 7–10 segments (446–639 MiB) again took 130–241 ms, against the 1 s
  budget, after a clean shutdown with a warm page cache. A store of two empty segments took about 130 ms too: the
  time is mostly reading the two unsealed 64 MiB segments whole, which a cold cache will lengthen.
- **Timer slack on this run.** The operating system added half of every timed wait (a 5 ms sleep woke 2.5 ms late).
  The writer's waits for `min_sync_interval` inherit it, so the 10 ms rows that wait on the bound are slower here
  than they would be without it (back to back: 79 commits a second instead of about 100). The load generator was
  built to stay on time under it (lag 0 in every row).

## Method

**What runs.** The harness opens a fresh store per writer setting with `WriterConfig::default()` (64 MiB segments
created ahead, 1 MiB batches, a queue of 65,536 entries, the disk marks), changing only `min_sync_interval` and the
mark sync, and starts `Log` on its two threads. The file system is `StdFs`, wrapped to time each sync by file
(segment, mark, directory); nothing in the writer changes. The store gets a fixed boot identity, so the timed mark
sync is in force where the row says so; the harness checks the writer's reported mode against the row's label.
Before each setup's loads one call is committed and the run waits 0.5 s, so the first segment and the one created
ahead exist.

**Latency** runs from the submit call to the acknowledgement. The acknowledgement is stamped on the writer thread:
the harness registers a waker on each call's `Pending`, and the writer wakes it as it completes the call, after the
batch's sync, the mark's move and, where required, the mark's sync. This is *ack* in 2.2's terms. The `design.md`
budget is to delivery to an in-process consumer; delivery is roadmap 5, and 2.2 found delivery at most 0.09 ms
above ack, so ack stands in for it here.

**Loads.** Each call holds one reading unless stated; a reading is an `f64` fact.

| Load | What it does | Full run |
|---|---|---|
| quiet | one writer, a call every 20–200 ms (pseudo-random, fixed seed): every call is the first after a quiet period | 40 s |
| steady *N* × 1 Hz | *N* series at 1 Hz, staggered evenly, one call per reading, open loop: *N* = 10, 100, 1,000 | 40, 20, 10 s |
| burst | every second, 500 calls submitted back to back | 20 s |
| back to back | one writer, each call submitted once the previous one is acknowledged | 10 s |
| saturation | four writers submitting calls of 100 readings as fast as back-pressure lets them | 5 s |

- Paced loads are open loop: the generator sends at fixed offsets, sleeping and then spinning, and records how late
  it sent (*Lag*). Latency counts from the submit call, so a late generator does not add to it but would bunch
  arrivals. Sleeps are split so that timer slack cannot make it late.
- A paced call that meets back-pressure is counted (*Busy*); none did.
- Every load runs under three settings: `min_sync_interval` 0 with the mark timed at 1 s; 10 ms (the default) with
  the mark timed; 10 ms with the mark synced at every commit.
- Percentiles are nearest rank; one needs enough calls to mean anything, so p90 is given from 10 calls, p99 from
  100 and p99.9 from 1,000. Calls in one batch wait for the same sync, so tails rest on the slowest few syncs.
- *Sync calls on their own* time each call through the same seam on an otherwise idle store: a 256-byte write
  then the sync, on a preallocated zero-filled file, for 2 s or 2,000 calls.
- *Opening the store again* times `StoreDir::open` and `LogWriter::open` after the clean shutdown that ends each
  setup: once with the same boot identity (rule 1) and once with another (rule 3, a recovery entry written).

**Code measured.** The engine as of the commit before this page's; later changes to recovery and the mark (a reset
stamp in the mark, rule 1's recovery entry) leave the commit path as measured, and a short run after them opened the
stores again without fault.

**Machine.** A 12-core ARM64 computer with its internal NVMe SSD, macOS 27.2, APFS, as in 2.2. The machine was in
ordinary use, load average 1.1–2.3. The run took 448 s at normal priority (lowering it was refused in the
environment the run was started from), one run of the full matrix after one earlier run that had found the timer
slack; the spread between runs was not measured this time (2.2: treat tails within about ±25 % as equal). Write
amplification was not measured again; 2.2's figures stand.

**Reproduce.**

```text
cargo run --release --locked -p kvik-log --example commit_latency -- <dir> --disk "internal NVMe SSD"   # about 8 minutes
cargo run --release --locked -p kvik-log --example commit_latency -- <dir> --short                      # about 2 minutes
```

`--only <load>` and `--repeat <n>` narrow or repeat the matrix. The run works in a `kvik-commit-latency`
subdirectory of `<dir>`, refuses `tmpfs` and `ramfs` (their sync does nothing), removes the stores at the end and
prints the tables below, with the environment: platform, file system, disk class (found where the system tells it,
or stated with `--disk`; never a model or serial), sync primitive, timer slack and load average.

## Against the budgets

`design.md` sets p50 ≤ 5 ms and p99 ≤ 20 ms for Linux on an NVMe SSD, with p50 ≤ 2 ms as a stretch goal on drives
with power-loss protection, and holds neither macOS nor Windows to them. Read against those figures, as 2.2 did,
for the default setting (10 ms, mark timed):

| Load | p50 ≤ 5 ms | p99 ≤ 20 ms | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| quiet, steady 10 × 1 Hz | yes (4.2) | yes (6.1–6.2) | the first reading after a quiet period is synced at once |
| steady 100 × 1 Hz | yes (4.1) | yes (14.1) | p90 12.0, most likely from timer slack (Summary); 5.2 p99 at 0 |
| steady 1000 × 1 Hz | no (8.3) | yes (13.4) | above 100 batches a second, as `design.md` says; 5.4 with the bound at 0 |
| burst of 500 | no (16.6) | yes (17.2) | the burst after the first batch waits for the interval, lengthened here by timer slack; 7.6 at 0 |
| back to back | no (13.0) | yes (15.0) | each commit waits for the interval, lengthened here by timer slack; 4.0 at 0 |
| saturation | no | no | queueing behind back-pressure, outside the budget's load statement |

- The stretch goal is out of reach on this machine: the sync alone takes 4.0 ms.
- With the mark synced at every commit p50 is about two syncs (8.1 ms) on every load, and p99 passes except in
  bursts (25.9 ms). That is the Windows configuration, which has no budget.
- Whether the budget holds on Linux depends on how fast the target's device flushes its cache. Nothing measured
  here decides it.

## Against the 2.2 spike

| | 2.2 spike (100/s, window 0) | 4.8 engine (steady 100 × 1 Hz, bound 0) | 2.2 spike (1,000/s, window 0) | 4.8 engine (steady 1000 × 1 Hz, bound 0) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| p50 ms | 3.93 | 4.17 | 5.71 | 5.36 |
| p99 ms | 4.86 | 5.22 | 8.17 | 8.38 |
| p99.9 ms | 8.91 | 9.00 | 12.68 | 12.41 |
| max ms | 9.61 | 9.48 | 16.04 | 16.39 |
| syncs/s | 100 | 100 | 288 | 265 |

- Both loads send one reading at a time, evenly spaced, 100 or 1,000 a second. The two agree within the spread 2.2 found between runs. The engine adds about
  0.2 ms at p50 (encoding, the mark's move, waking the caller).
- The sync calls cost what they did in 2.2 (`F_FULLFSYNC` p50 3.98–4.00 ms, creating a 64 MiB segment 35–38 ms).
- 2.2's ceiling, 7.2 million readings a second, came from one generator emitting single readings straight into the
  spike's queue; the engine's 5.3 million comes from four writers with calls of 100 readings. Both sit far above any expected load.

## Cloud runners (Linux and Windows)

The workflow [`bench.yml`](../../.github/workflows/bench.yml) runs the harness on `ubuntu-latest` and
`windows-latest`, started by hand (`workflow_dispatch`, short or full), and uploads each OS's tables as an artifact,
labelled as a cloud virtual machine. Hosted runners have virtual disks whose flush semantics the runner does not
state, so their figures show how the engine behaves there and nothing about the budget or the device cache. They
are not recorded here yet: the workflow has not run. To record them, run the workflow and add the artifacts' tables
under this heading with the run's date and commit.

## Pending: the Linux NVMe target

This is the step that settles the budget check. On the Linux machine with the NVMe disk:

```text
scripts/bench-nvme.sh <dir on the NVMe file system> --disk "NVMe, power-loss protection: yes|no"
```

It builds the harness, runs the full matrix three times (about 25 minutes) at normal priority, and writes the
tables to `docs/measurements/4.8-linux-nvme-<date>.md`. Then:
1. Commit that file and add a section here: the budget table above with the Linux figures (p50 ≤ 5 ms and p99 ≤
   20 ms at the default setting, the stretch goal if the drive has power-loss protection), and the spread over the
   three runs.
2. Check the environment table: `fdatasync`, a file system that syncs (ext4 or XFS, not `tmpfs`), the disk class
   and its write-cache mode, and timer slack near zero.
3. Look at the 100 × 1 Hz row at 10 ms: with timer slack near zero its p90 and p99 should fall to near the sync if
   the tail here came from slack. If p50 there is above 5 ms, or p99 near 20 ms, raise it at gate 4.9.

Also left for real hardware, not for this harness: the trials of the questions below that need a device that fails
writes, a full copy-on-write file system or power cuts, a restart from a cold page cache after a crash, and write
amplification on Linux.

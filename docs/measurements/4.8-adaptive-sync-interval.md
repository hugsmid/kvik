# The adaptive batch-sync interval: commit latency rows

The rows behind the default `log.sync_interval_floor` of 2 ms (Q-710) and the multiplier of 8 (Q-712), from the
harness of roadmap 4.8 ([`log/examples/commit_latency.rs`](../../log/examples/commit_latency.rs), method in
[4.8-commit-latency.md](4.8-commit-latency.md)). **Indicative only**: short runs on a shared virtual machine with
four virtual CPUs, ext4 on a virtio disk, while another build ran on the same host; not the Linux NVMe target, which
confirms them before gate 4.9.

## Summary

- **Sync time.** A batch's `fdatasync` took about 0.4–0.6 ms here, so the interval settled at about 3–5 ms (eight
  times the smoothed sync time), above the 2 ms floor; under saturation, where a batch is 1 MiB, it stayed near the
  10 ms ceiling.
- **Where it helps** (medians, fixed 10 ms against adaptive): 1,000 calls a second p50 5.47 → 2.35 ms; 16 writers at
  200 calls a second p50 6.00 → 0.52 ms (bound 0: 0.40–0.43 ms); one writer back to back p50 9.95 → 3.66 ms at twice
  the commits a second; one writer of 2,000-reading calls 101 → 210 calls a second; bursts p50 5.54 → 2.96 ms.
- **No regression beyond run-to-run spread.** Saturation p50, p99 and throughput are within the fixed runs' range;
  the medians are equal or better except the p50 at 16 × 10/call, 25.82 ms against 25.53 ms (about 1 % worse). The
  quiet, 10 × 1 Hz and 100 × 1 Hz loads sync every call on arrival under either setting, so their schedules are the
  same; their medians differ by 0.02–0.08 ms, inside the fixed runs' own range. One adaptive run each of 10 × 1 Hz
  and of the burst load met a stall of the virtual disk (a 310 ms p99, a 177 ms sync); a fixed saturation run met one
  of 365 ms. One writer back to back has a p99 of 10.0–14.0 ms against 10.4–12.3 ms: a slow sync lifts the interval
  to the ceiling for a few syncs, so its tail is the fixed schedule's.
- **Slow devices.** With 5 or 20 ms added to every `sync_data` the interval stayed at 10 ms in every row and log
  syncs a second never exceeded the fixed rows'; the differences between the two settings there, on the same
  schedule, show the spread of this machine (saturation throughput up to 10 %).
- **Rate bound.** Log syncs a second stayed below 1/floor (500) in every row: at most 269 with the multiplier 8, 476
  with 4.
- **Multiplier sweep.** 4 halves the wait again (back to back p50 2.0 ms, 1,000 calls a second 1.3–1.7 ms) for about
  1.7 times the syncs of 8 at 1,000 calls a second and the lowest saturation throughput of the three; 16 stays near
  the fixed schedule. 8 is kept (Q-712).
- **The design's marks.** Saturation p99 no worse than fixed, the delayed rows no more syncs than fixed, and log
  syncs never above 1/floor: met. 16 writers at 200 calls a second within 25 % of bound 0: borderline (0.45 and 0.59
  ms against 0.40–0.43). One writer of 2,000-reading calls within 5 % of bound 0: not met (210 against about 1,370
  calls a second), because this machine encodes such a call in well under a millisecond, so the interval and not the
  writer's work sets the pace. The owner ruled the 2 ms default without these marks (Q-710).

## Method

`--short` runs, niced, each started only while the one-minute load average was under 2.0, alternating the fixed and
adaptive settings: two rounds of every load under fixed 10 ms (twice per round), adaptive with multipliers 8, 4 and
16 (a build per multiplier; the multiplier is a constant), and bound 0; four more alternating pairs of the saturation
and burst loads and three of the quiet and 10 × 1 Hz loads; one pair at each sync delay. All settings time the mark
on its 1 s timer. The "16 writers" load is sent open loop from one generator thread, as sixteen periodic sources at
their own phases, not from sixteen threads. "Interval at end" and "Sync time" are `LogStats::sync_interval` and
`sync_time` after the load. Ranges are over runs.
